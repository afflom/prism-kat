What are we paying for, and what could we lose?

Two costs matter: storage you keep and do not need, and data you lose and did need. This Prism keeps both visible. Storage is reclaimed only when nothing still needs it, and a rollout never goes ahead without a complete backup.

  2. Only part of the data is backed up: hold until it all is.

The decisions behind this view

GC eviction

Decision table (OMG DMN 1.5, hit policy FIRST) · grounded in UOR-Foundation/kappa-distribution

Decide whether garbage collection may evict a blob from a kappa-distribution registry, so storage is reclaimed without losing anything a tag, pin, finalizer or upload still needs.

A request is tag (whether a tag binding reaches the blob in the sweep snapshot), pin (the blob's pin: TTL 0, an unexpired TTL, an expired TTL, or no pin), finalizer (a finalizer pin and whether its controller released it), upload (whether an open upload session pin protects the content), in that order.

Promises the Lean kernel proves for every request

  • It never answers evict when finalizer is outstanding: an outstanding finalizer always blocks eviction.
  • It never answers evict when tag is bound: a tagged blob is never evicted.
  • It always answers evict when tag is none and pin is expired and finalizer is not outstanding and upload is none: an untagged blob whose pin expired is always reclaimed.

Rollout gate

Decision table (OMG DMN 1.5, hit policy FIRST) · grounded in UOR-Foundation/kappa-registry

Tell an engineering leader, in one word, whether a content-addressed platform is ready to roll out: go, hold or no-go.

A request is conformance (the conformance verdict of the registry), key (whether the node's Ed25519 signing key in keys/ is backed up), backup (whether blobs/ and state.redb are backed up), env (whether the toolchain is pinned by a Nix flake lock), images (whether every image the rollout needs was admitted), in that order.

Promises the Lean kernel proves for every request

  • It never answers go when key is missing: never roll out a node whose identity could be lost.
  • It never answers go when conformance is untested: never roll out an untested registry.
  • It never answers go when backup is not complete: never roll out without a complete backup.
  • It always answers no-go when conformance is nonconformant: a nonconformant registry is always no-go.
