Are we ready to roll out? Go, hold or no-go.

You need one word and the reason for it. The rollout gate combines the other decisions: is the registry conformant, is its identity key backed up, is the data backed up, is the build reproducible, were the images admitted. It never says go when the key or a backup is missing, and that is proved, not promised.

Walk it

  1. Everything checks out: go.

    rollout-gate ideal backed-up complete pinned admitted model answer: go

  2. Nothing is broken, but nothing is proven: hold.

    rollout-gate untested backed-up complete pinned admitted model answer: hold

  3. The signing key is not backed up: no-go.

    rollout-gate ideal missing complete pinned admitted model answer: no-go

The decisions behind this view

Rollout gate

Decision table (OMG DMN 1.5, hit policy FIRST) · grounded in UOR-Foundation/kappa-registry

Tell an engineering leader, in one word, whether a content-addressed platform is ready to roll out: go, hold or no-go.

A request is conformance (the conformance verdict of the registry), key (whether the node's Ed25519 signing key in keys/ is backed up), backup (whether blobs/ and state.redb are backed up), env (whether the toolchain is pinned by a Nix flake lock), images (whether every image the rollout needs was admitted), in that order.

Promises the Lean kernel proves for every request

  • It never answers go when key is missing: never roll out a node whose identity could be lost.
  • It never answers go when conformance is untested: never roll out an untested registry.
  • It never answers go when backup is not complete: never roll out without a complete backup.
  • It always answers no-go when conformance is nonconformant: a nonconformant registry is always no-go.
